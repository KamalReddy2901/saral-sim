Saral simulationThe barrier and the learner
One clearly identified barrier

Standard study text is written for readers who can follow long, abstract sentences

The barrier

Textbook passages use long sentences, several ideas at once and hard words, with no picture support. An easy-read version of a given passage is not available on demand, least of all without internet.

Who it is for

Learners with Down syndrome who benefit from simplified text and visual support, using the device directly. Learners differ widely, so we assume no single reading level: text size and contrast are adjustable.

Why these design choices

Guidance for teachers says print should be bigger and bolder (Bird, SEN Magazine 80). Easy-to-Read standards ask for short sentences, one idea each (Inclusion Europe).

Input

One educational passage of about 150–300 words (the organiser’s passage on the day).

Output 1 and 2

An easy-read summary of 5–7 sentences, and 3 or more yes/no or two-choice questions, each answerable from the text on screen.

Output 3

A highlighted key word per sentence and a verified pictogram where an exact match exists. No picture beats a wrong picture.

No cloud AI inference. Wi-Fi off, Ethernet unplugged, with an offline-check script as proof.4 / 11
Saral simulationTrust: AI proposes, code verifies
Chapter 7 in the simulation

A small model can slip, so nothing reaches the learner unchecked

Check A: the easy-read text

Counts words per sentence, estimates reading level, and confirms names, numbers and key terms come from the passage.

Check B: the questions

Every question must have a valid answer that can be found in the text on screen. Weak questions are dropped.

When the AI fails

Retry up to twice, then fall back to selected original sentences and template questions, so three verified questions always appear.

These are rough indicators, not proof of accessibility or complete factual correctness. We report failures.

The walkthrough: Check A finds a 19-word sentence (limit 12). The retry fixes it.

What we will measure on the Pi

Model, quantization, file size · peak memory (model alone, and with the screen) · time per stage and total, tokens per second · average and longest sentence, reading grade before and after · first-attempt check pass rate, fallback use, pictogram coverage.

How we measure

Real Pi, Wi-Fi off. A warm-up, then three runs per passage, reported as ranges. At least five passages from different subjects and a two-person quality review. Figures in the simulation are placeholders until then.

Saral simulationModel, dependencies and risks
Feasibility

A small model on a Pi, with a plan for what could go wrong

Model and runtime (examples to confirm)

  • Primary: Llama 3.2 1B Instruct
  • Backup: Qwen3 0.6B or SmolLM2
  • Research option: Gemma 3 1B, only if its ExecuTorch route is verified
  • Quantized for XNNPACK; the exact scheme (for example 8-bit activations, 4-bit weights) is finalised after testing
  • Choice order: organiser-approved, memory fit with the screen running, format reliability, speed

To confirm before the build

  • Approved model list and Raspberry Pi model, RAM, OS
  • ExecuTorch version and export requirements
  • Display and other hardware; Linux dev environment

Top risks

  • Broken format or long sentences: few-shot examples, targeted retries, extractive fallback
  • Slow or memory-heavy: smaller model, capped tokens, terminal path always works
Still to be confirmed with the organisers: approved models, Raspberry Pi model and display.9 / 11
Saral simulationWhat we do and do not claim
Trustworthy accessibility

Careful claims, backed by what the research does and does not show

We do not claim

  • No learning-outcome claims. We have not tested with learners.
  • Not certified Easy-to-Read. That needs review by people with intellectual disabilities, which is planned.
  • The faithfulness check works at word level and cannot catch every meaning error.

A 2026 systematic review found the evidence that Easy Read improves comprehension is limited and mostly low quality. So we claim only measurable output quality, and we plan validation with special educators.
