Why Saral stands apart

Not another offline summariser: a lesson built for one learner group

1A specific learner, not “students”

Designed around learners with Down syndrome and Easy-to-Read principles: short sentences, one idea each, concrete words.

2A lesson, not just text

Key word and a verified pictogram per sentence, on a large-print screen with one idea per screen. The screen is core, not a bonus.

3Trust by construction

AI proposes, code verifies. Plain-code checks and a safe fallback mean three verified questions always appear. We state what we do not claim.

4You can judge it before the event

A full 3D simulation of the device, offline proof and checks, with honest Simulated/Measured labels, and private by design: nothing leaves the device.

The barrier

Standard study text uses long, abstract sentences with no picture support, and an easy-read version of a given passage is not available on demand, least of all offline.

The learner

People with Down syndrome who benefit from simplified text and visual support. Needs differ widely, so Saral adjusts text size and contrast instead of assuming one reading level.

The five mandatory deliverables
RequirementHow Saral meets it
1 · Barrier, learners, approachStandard text is hard to read and has no visual support; easy-read text with pictograms for learners with Down syndrome, on the device.
2 · Passage, approved model, ExecuTorch on PiQuantized small instruct model via ExecuTorch (XNNPACK), run on the organiser’s passage. Final model subject to organiser approval and compatibility testing.
3 · Summary + 3 quiz questions5–7 easy-read sentences and 3–4 yes/no or two-choice questions. Terminal output always works; the screen is an extra.
4 · No cloud; time, model, sizeWi-Fi off, Ethernet unplugged, offline-check script. Time per stage, model and file size, peak memory with and without the screen.
5 · Impact exampleA before/after sentence from the organiser’s passage with word counts and the picture used. Example: 16 words and two ideas become 8 words and one idea, with a picture.

How we measure on the Pi

  • Model, quantization, file size; peak memory
  • Time per stage and total, tokens per second
  • Sentence length, reading grade before and after
  • Check pass rate, fallback use, pictogram coverage
  • Warm-up, then 3 runs per passage, as ranges; 5+ passages; two-person quality review

Model and runtime (examples to confirm)

  • Primary: Llama 3.2 1B Instruct
  • Backup: Qwen3 0.6B or SmolLM2
  • Gemma 3 1B only if its ExecuTorch route is verified
  • Quantization scheme finalised after testing
  • To confirm: approved list, Pi model, RAM, OS, display

What we do not claim

  • No learning-outcome claims. Not tested with learners; a 2026 systematic review found Easy Read comprehension evidence limited.
  • Not certified Easy-to-Read. Needs review by people with intellectual disabilities (planned).
  • Checks are rough indicators and cannot catch every meaning error. We report failures.
